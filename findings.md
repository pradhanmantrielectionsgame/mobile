## 2026-07-28 — Changing `game.winner` alone wasn't enough to make hung parliament read as a draw — the end screen had its own independent, redundant check
**Finding:** `mobile/main.js`'s `showEndOverlay()` computed its hung-parliament headline from `var vsAI = game.players.p2.isAI; headline = vsAI ? 'Hung parliament — you lose' : 'Hung parliament';` — it never actually read `game.winner` for this branch, it independently re-derived the same "AI wins ties" conclusion ADR-0006 encoded in `finalizeGame()`. Fixing `finalizeGame()` alone (setting `game.winner = 'draw'` unconditionally) would have left the end screen still showing "you lose" on every hung parliament, since this UI code path never consulted `game.winner` in the first place.
**Context:** Found while implementing ADR-0010 (hung parliament is always a draw, superseding ADR-0006) — a grep for `vsAI`/`isAI` in `main.js` turned up the duplicate logic before it shipped as a silent regression.
**Implication:** When changing what a `game.winner`/game-state outcome means, grep for every place that independently re-derives the same conclusion, not just where `game.winner` itself is read — this project has hit this "two places must agree" failure mode repeatedly (CLAUDE.md documents several: agenda effect text vs. real benefits/costs, the `[hidden]` CSS attribute rule, etc.) and this is another instance of the same pattern.

## 2026-07-28 — `balance-sim.js`'s reported win rates were almost entirely a measurement artifact: 76% of games hit a hung parliament, and the game's own tie-break rule always awarded it to p2
**Finding:** Checking the existing `mobile/balance-log.jsonl`, 1,595/2,100 games (76.0%) ended in a hung parliament (neither side reached the 272-seat majority). The real game's own rule (`finalizeGame()`, ADR-0006) resolves a hung parliament as `game.winner = game.players.p2.isAI ? 'p2' : 'draw'` — correct for a human-vs-AI match, but in every simulated game p2 was always the AI slot, so p1 (whichever politician was being measured) structurally lost 76% of games by rule alone, regardless of kit quality. This was a far bigger driver of the near-0% win rates across most of the roster than the naive-p1-vs-full-AI-p2 strategy gap that was initially suspected.
**Context:** User asked "how can the win rate for so many be 0 percent" after a first re-ranking by win rate (not avg seats, per user's correction) showed 13 of 21 politicians at 0-3%.
**Implication:** Any future win-rate measurement from this simulator must either (a) treat hung parliament as a neutral outcome (this session scored it 0.5/0.5 in `balance-sim.js`'s own tally, without touching the real game's ADR-0006 rule) or (b) explicitly account for its ~76-95%-per-politician frequency before drawing conclusions from a raw win/loss count.

## 2026-07-28 — `mobile/game.js`'s AI logic (`aiStep`/`runAIFull` and their scoring helpers) was hardcoded to player key 'p2' throughout, not just at the top level
**Finding:** Beyond the previously-known `aiStep`/`runAIFull` hardcoding, the helper functions they call — `pickAIPowerTarget`, `pickAICompletedAgenda`, `groupFocusBonus`, `scoreInvestState`, `bestInPool`, `pickAIInvestmentTarget` — all had 'p1'/'p2' literals baked into their logic (e.g. `scoreInvestState`'s tie-break term was hardcoded `game.pop[s.svgId].p1 - game.pop[s.svgId].p2`, assuming the actor is always p2 and the opponent always p1). All were generalized to take explicit `playerKey`/`oppKey` parameters, with `aiStep`/`runAIFull` defaulting `playerKey` to `'p2'` so every existing call site (`main.js`, `simulate.js`, which never pass a second argument) behaves identically to before.
**Context:** Needed to let `mobile/balance-sim.js` drive p1 with the real AI too (a symmetric AI-vs-AI match), rather than the naive/random strategy it used previously — the project's own prior finding had flagged the object-swap approach as unsafe but hadn't yet built the proper generalized version.
**Implication:** A new `Game.setupAI(game, playerKey, rng)` export now flags an arbitrary seat as AI-controlled (profile + target group), matching what `createGame` already did for p2. Any future headless test needing a second AI-controlled seat should use this rather than reinventing naive-strategy stand-ins.

## 2026-07-28 — Once measured correctly, 4 of 21 politicians (the 3 official celebrities plus "Sivaji Rao") had both an unusually strong agenda kit AND an unusually strong/free special power stacked together
**Finding:** Amitabh Bachchan, Hema Malini, Rajinikanth, and Sivaji Rao all had agenda-kit national averages (seat-weighted avg pts per full tap, summed across their 4 agendas) of 13-16, among the highest in the 21-politician roster, on top of their already-strong special powers. Sivaji Rao's "One-Day Ordinance" (`data/politicians-data.json`) had literally zero cost of any kind while instantly setting a 48-seat state (Maharashtra) to 100% popularity — the single most broken power in the roster.
**Context:** User clarified that their original "celebrities should be OP" intent meant the special power specifically (since these entries "don't have the traditional politician appeal thematically"), not the agenda kit — the double-stacking was an unintentional tuning error from an earlier session, not the design intent.
**Implication:** Rebalancing an over/underperforming politician should first check whether the agenda kit and the special power are both pulling in the same direction before assuming either one alone is the fix — and for personas whose agenda kit is already a deliberate thematic match (confirmed true for all 4 here after a reverted first attempt to swap agendas), the special power's cost/timing/magnitude is the correct lever, not the agenda kit.

## 2026-07-28 — A power-magnitude nerf can appear to backfire when compared across simulation batches where other politicians' kits also changed ("strength of schedule" confound)
**Finding:** Cutting Hema Malini's power from 8%/16% (nationwide/home) to 2%/16% appeared to raise her measured win rate from 60.0% to 64.5% in a batch where 2-3 of her 20 simulated opponents (Sivaji Rao, Rajinikanth) were also weakened in the same run. Re-testing with her power alone toggled, opponent pool held exactly constant, showed the true effect was a genuine -13-point drop (77.5% with the old power → 64.5% with the new one) — the batch-level comparison had been confounded by her opponents getting weaker at the same time.
**Context:** User pushed back that the result "makes no sense" for a change that was strictly weaker in every state except an unchanged home bonus.
**Implication:** Any future single-politician balance change should be verified with an isolated test (only that politician's data toggled, everyone else held at the current committed state) before trusting a before/after comparison across two full batch runs, since `balance-sim.js`'s win rate is relative to the whole 20-opponent pool, not an absolute measure of one kit.

## 2026-07-28 — A magnitude-only nerf hits diminishing returns; breaking through required combining it with a much later `requiresMinPhase` gate in the same edit
**Finding:** Cutting Amitabh Bachchan's "Celebrity Endorsement" nationwide magnitude from 12%→10% dropped his win rate 78%→71% (-7pts), but the next equal-sized cut (10%→8%) only moved it 71%→67.5% (-3.5pts) — diminishing returns on the same lever. An earlier attempt to fix him via cost+timing alone (₹1,000cr→₹2,000cr + `requiresMinPhase: 3`, magnitude unchanged at 12%) moved his win rate by exactly 0%. The combination that finally broke through was magnitude 8%→6% **together with** pushing `requiresMinPhase` from 3 to 6 in the same edit, dropping him 67.5%→62%.
**Context:** User's target was closing the gap between Bachchan and the rest of the top cluster (Rajinikanth 58.5%, Hema Malini 60%) without eliminating his "hardest to beat" status entirely.
**Implication:** For this politician's specific power shape (nationwide, "Others"-sourced, no per-region interaction), a single lever pushed further has diminishing effect — a small early-gate `requiresMinPhase` value (3, i.e. usable almost immediately) contributes far less than a late-game one (6, roughly the midpoint of the 10-phase match) since it cuts off several extra phases of compounding benefit. Test cost/timing/magnitude changes in combination, not just escalating one lever repeatedly, if an earlier single-lever version underperformed expectations.

## 2026-07-28 — The existing single-player engine architecture is already well-shaped for real-time human-vs-human networking, with almost no engine changes needed
**Finding:** `mobile/game.js`'s action functions (`investCash`, `playRallyToken`, `tapAgenda`, `activatePower`, etc.) are already pure, side-effect-free, playerKey-agnostic mutators — `(game, playerKey, ...args) → {ok, reason?}`, no DOM/sound/console access. The AI opponent (`aiStep`) is not a separate code path; it calls these exact same functions with `playerKey='p2'`. Separately, `mobile/engine.js`'s `resolveSimultaneousGain` already exists specifically to reconcile two players' actions landing on the same state in the same phase — the engine already assumes concurrent two-sided play, it has just never been fed by a second live human player.
**Context:** User asked how to extend the game to real-time 2-player-over-internet play. A research pass over `game.js`/`main.js`/`engine.js` (plus pre-existing ADR-0001/0002/0007 on deferred live matchmaking) found these properties already in place rather than needing to be designed from scratch.
**Implication:** A networked multiplayer layer can be built as an additive `mobile/net.js` module that publishes/replays actions through the existing `G.*` functions (an event-sourced action log), without modifying `game.js`/`engine.js` internals. Full architecture written to `design/multiplayer-implementation-plan.md`. The one real gap is determinism: `game.rng` is currently seeded with unshareable `Math.random` and needs a swappable seeded PRNG for two clients to reach identical starting positions.

## 2026-07-28 — Starting-position seat variance: the stopping-rule threshold controls the mean without widening the spread
**Finding:** `mobile/engine.js`'s `generateStartingPosition()` (pre-fix) produced starting national seats averaging ~135-142 with a right tail reaching 180-200+ for one player — confirmed via 3,000-8,000-sample direct simulation, with ~46-47.5% of games putting at least one player over 150 seats (not a rare tail, close to a coin flip). Raising the advantage-draw stopping threshold (`drawStopAt`/`drawBudget`, 100/130 → 124/154) shifted the mean up to ~150 while leaving the standard deviation essentially unchanged (~12.4-12.7 across the whole tested range) — because the stopping rule is self-correcting: a player who draws a run of small states just needs more draws to cross the threshold, which keeps the overshoot (and therefore σ) stable regardless of where the threshold sits.
**Context:** User reported seeing 180-200-seat starts in actual play and initially proposed narrowing the baseline population range (500-2900 bps) as a fix; a parameter sweep showed narrowing the baseline instead just drags the average down without independently controlling the tail. Direct mean/σ measurement (not inferred from percentiles) was needed to find that the advantage-draw threshold is the one knob that moves the mean without stretching the spread, letting a plain reject-and-retry hard cap (tried first) be replaced with pure parameter tuning once the user's actual target — mean 150, σ~12.5 (175/125 as 2σ events) — was made precise.
