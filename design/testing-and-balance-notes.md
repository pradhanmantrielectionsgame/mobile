# Testing & balance-sim notes

Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` 2026-08-21 (was always-loaded; only matters when doing dev-server/Playwright/balance-sim work). Referenced from `CLAUDE.md`'s "Local development & testing" and "Game design principles" sections — read this when that work comes up.

## Balance-sim methodology

- **For politician/agenda balance questions, run `mobile/balance-sim.js`** (originally: every politician as p1 with a fixed naive strategy against every other politician's real AI as p2; **updated 2026-07-28: both seats now play as real AI** via `Game.setupAI(game, 'p1', rng)` + `Game.runAIFull(game, 'p1'/'p2')`, since a naive-vs-AI strategy gap was swamping the kit-quality signal — across N seeds per ordered pair, logging results to `mobile/balance-log.jsonl`) **rather than relying on manual small-sample play.** Built 2026-07-28 after a small manual-play sample suggested Rahul Gandhi, Mamata Banerjee, and Manmohan Singh felt harder to win with — a 1,260-game batch run confirmed all three as genuinely bottom-tier (avg final seats 172.5 / 170.7 / 196.5 vs. Hema Malini's roster-best 261.7), each for a different structural reason (see `findings.md`). Re-run after any politician/agenda tweak (`node mobile/balance-sim.js 5`, or higher for more confidence) to check whether it actually moved the needle. **Read win rate, not avg final seats, as the primary metric** — avg seats doesn't map cleanly onto "how often does this kit actually win," which is what balance decisions should target. A hung parliament (48–98% of games depending on the politician) is scored as a 0.5/0.5 draw in the tally, matching the real game's own rule (see `CLAUDE.md`'s hung-parliament bullet).
- **Two methodology traps found rebalancing the roster to a target win-rate band (2026-07-28):** (1) A single-politician change must be verified with an **isolated test** — that politician's data toggled, every other politician's data held at the exact same committed state — before trusting a before/after win-rate comparison across two full batch runs. Comparing across batches where *other* politicians' kits also changed in the same run is confounded by "strength of schedule" (a nerf can appear to backfire — win rate went *up* — purely because 2-3 of that politician's 20 simulated opponents got weaker in the same batch; an isolated re-test recovered the real, expected direction). (2) **A single lever (cost, timing, or magnitude alone) pushed further hits diminishing returns, and cost/timing changes can move a power's win rate by exactly 0% on their own** (confirmed on Amitabh Bachchan's "Others"-sourced nationwide power) — test combinations (e.g. a magnitude cut **together with** pushing `requiresMinPhase` deep into the match, not just an early gate) rather than escalating one lever repeatedly if an earlier single-lever attempt underperforms.
- **A third trap, confirmed 2026-08-21 on Narendra Modi's Demonetization: `balance-sim.js` win rates systematically undervalue *conditional/exploit-dependent* powers relative to *unconditional/guaranteed* ones.** Demonetization is pure denial (freezes the opponent's funds, no direct benefit to Modi) — two independent single-lever changes (cheaper: -5%→-2% popularity cost; longer: 1 phase→2 phases) each moved his win rate by ~0%, because the power's entire payoff depends on the AI *noticing and capitalizing on* the opponent's frozen phase, and the AI (a greedy heuristic, not adversarially tuned) doesn't reliably do that. Adding a flat, unconditional +4% nationwide popularity benefit alongside the freeze moved his win rate 8 points immediately (43.5%→54%) — same craft-cost, same gate, only the *shape* changed from conditional to guaranteed. **Before nerfing or writing off as weak any power whose value depends on smart timing/targeting rather than an instant guaranteed effect (Yogi's targeted Bulldozer Action, Nehru's contingent Non-Alignment are the other clear candidates), treat its measured win rate as a likely floor, not its true ceiling in human play** — a skilled human opponent can exploit a conditional power far more reliably than this AI does.

## Local development & testing

- **Forcing a specific matchup for playtesting — requires `PLAYTEST_BUILD = true` in `mobile/main.js`** (next to `UNLOCK_ALL`, same rules: local only, flip it back before committing). Two ways in, both gated on that flag:
  - **In-app (works on a phone): long-press the version label on the welcome screen for ~0.6s.** Badge gains `+playtest`, every politician becomes playable regardless of unlock state, charges/cooldowns are ignored, and picking your politician leads to a second pick for the opponent instead of starting — so any of the 21×20 pairings is two taps, including the same-party ones the normal draw excludes. The flag lives in `localStorage` (`pme_playtest`), so it survives reloads and home-screen launches. Long-press again to turn it off. A short tap on the same label still cycles the forced AI profile, unchanged.
  - **URL: `?vs=<p1>,<p2>`** (e.g. `?vs=tendulkar,ambedkar`). Either side takes a full `id` or any unambiguous fragment — `modi`, `indira`, `narasimha` resolve; `gandhi` (3 matches) and `rao` (2) resolve to nothing rather than guessing. `?p1=<id>&p2=<id>` works too. **Don't rely on either on a phone.** Three separate forced-matchup links sent to a real device (2026-09-03) all arrived with the entire query string missing — a home-screen launch uses `manifest.json`'s bare `./index.html` `start_url`, and links get mangled in transit besides (the `?ai=` hook carries its own note about the same loss). `?vs=` exists because it has no `&` to be truncated at, but the in-app long-press is the reliable path. **The badge is the diagnostic**: bare `v<x.y.z>` = the query string never arrived; `+modi→gandhi` while facing someone random = the token arrived but didn't resolve.
- **Why the gate exists.** Forcing the *human's* politician hands a player someone they haven't unlocked, and the long-press unlocks the whole roster — so ungated, both would be an accidental progression bypass on the live site, discoverable by a curious player prodding the footer or by pasting a shared link. The unlock progression is deliberately client-side and devtools-bypassable (see `CLAUDE.md`), but that's a considered "anyone determined can", not "anyone curious will". `?p2=` and `?ai=` stay **ungated** — they only choose your opponent or the AI's difficulty and never reach a locked politician, which is the property the gate restores.
- **`scripts/deploy-mobile.js` refuses to deploy while `UNLOCK_ALL` or `PLAYTEST_BUILD` is true** (`assertPlaytestFlagsOff`, runs first in `main()`, exits 1). Nothing else catches this: `npm test` and `npm run serve` both read `mobile/`, where the flag being on is the whole point, so the mistake is invisible until it's live. The check uses regex **literals** — a first attempt built them with `new RegExp('var\s+' + name + ...)`, whose escaping collapsed to `vars+NAME`, matching nothing and waving the deploy straight through. A guard that fails open is worse than no guard; if you edit it, verify it by flipping a flag on and confirming the deploy actually exits 1.
- Forced/playtest matches are **unrated** (`game.ratedMatch = false`) so they can't drift the adaptive AI level, and **spend no charge**, so testing one pairing repeatedly can't burn a politician into a 6h cooldown.

- **`mobile/manifest.json`'s `start_url` controls where iOS Safari's "Add to Home Screen" actually launches, regardless of which page under its scope you tapped the button from.** Any page under `mobile/` that links a manifest gets a home-screen icon pinned to that manifest's `start_url`, not the page it was added from. Confirmed 2026-07-27 debugging a report that Safari kept reopening the old build after saving a new prototype page to the home screen. Any future prototype/experiment page under `mobile/` needs its own dedicated manifest file (e.g. `manifest-redesign-a.json`) linked via its own `<link rel="manifest">` if it must be independently installable — don't let it inherit the real `manifest.json`.
- `mobile/index.html` cannot be published as a self-contained Claude Artifact as-is, unlike the frozen `pme-mobile-sheet.html` prototype — it loads `engine.js`/`game.js`/`main.js` as separate `<script src>` files and `game.js` `fetch()`s JSON data files from disk at runtime, none of which an Artifact's sandbox can resolve. Getting the real engine onto an Artifact would need a bundling step first (inline the JS files, inline the JSON data, shim `fetch`) — not done as of 2026-07-23; local-server access is the current path instead.
- **`npm run serve`'s static handler only resolves an exact `/` request, not a bare directory path like `/mobile/`** (only `/` gets substituted to `/mobile/index.html`; a request for the directory itself 404s). Any service worker or other `caches.addAll`-style precache list must list explicit filenames only, never a bare `'./'` entry — `caches.addAll` fails atomically on the first 404, so the whole install silently never completes and `navigator.serviceWorker.ready` hangs forever with no visible error. Hit this building `mobile/sw.js` 2026-07-23.
- **After any change to AI investment aggressiveness or behavior (e.g. `mobile/game.js`'s AI personality profiles), re-run `npm test` plus a stress test that specifically hammers cheap/tiny states, not just the normal test suite.** A more aggressive AI can drive a state to exactly 100% ownership for the first time, which can surface latent redistribution-engine edge cases (a proportional-split branch with a zero denominator) that ordinary play never reaches — this is exactly how a real invariant bug in `engine.js`'s `loseAt()` was found 2026-07-23, undetected until then despite the existing test suite passing throughout.
- **Headless Node-based AI testing (`mobile/simulate.js`, or any ad-hoc script that calls `G.runAIFull()`) is an upper bound on AI strength, not a match for the real browser opponent.** `runAIFull()` has no pacing limit — it resolves a whole phase's worth of actions instantly. The real browser AI is throttled via `mobile/main.js`'s `scheduleAITick()`/`planAITickPacing()` to act at a human-comparable pace across the phase. A throttled-vs-unthrottled comparison found the throttled AI leaving up to 68% of its lifetime budget unspent in some seeds before this was fixed (2026-07-25) — any future AI-strength conclusion drawn from headless simulation alone should be treated as provisional until checked against, or explicitly caveated against, the real paced behavior.
- **When modeling a special power's real value, an instant before/after `nationalSeats()` diff only measures effects that touch `game.pop` directly — it's blind to funds/token transfers, which have zero immediate seat effect and only pay off over the phases that follow.** Concrete break: testing Indira Gandhi's National Emergency (seizes the opponent's cash + rally tokens, costs -10% of her own popularity nationwide) as an instant swing measured -52.7 seats — which looks like the seizure is actively harmful, when really the instant-diff test only ever sees the popularity-cost side; the seizure's actual value (denying the opponent's investment budget, handing Indira more of it) only shows up across the rest of the match. The correct test forks an identical game state into "power used" vs "power skipped" branches at the point of activation, plays both out to the end with normal AI/investment on both sides, and compares final seats — that's what found -10% was a genuine trap (-22.5 true seats) while -5% breaks even (+2.3). Found and fixed 2026-07-26 (numbers later revised again the same day after a related confiscation-vs-transfer correction — see `docs/wiki.html`'s Special powers section for the current values).
- **Never simulate "p1 also plays like the AI" in a headless test by swapping `game.players.p1`/`.p2` object references to redirect the AI's targeting.** `game.pop[svgId].p1`/`.p2` are fixed string keys independent of which politician object currently occupies which player slot, so the swap credits investment gains to the wrong pop bucket while still deducting the swapped-in player's funds — a plausible-looking but false result. Found 2026-07-26 while building a power-value test for Tendulkar's floor mechanic; it produced a wrong "value increases over time" conclusion before being caught. **Update 2026-07-28: `mobile/game.js`'s `aiStep`/`runAIFull` (and their scoring helpers — `pickAIPowerTarget`, `pickAICompletedAgenda`, `groupFocusBonus`, `scoreInvestState`, `bestInPool`, `pickAIInvestmentTarget`) no longer hardcode `'p2'`** — they take an explicit `playerKey`/`oppKey`, defaulting to `'p2'` so every pre-existing call site (`main.js`, `simulate.js`) is unaffected. A new `Game.setupAI(game, playerKey, rng)` export flags an arbitrary seat as AI-controlled (profile + target group), the same way `createGame` already did for p2 — use this (`Game.setupAI(game, 'p1', rng)` then `Game.runAIFull(game, 'p1')`) for a real symmetric AI-vs-AI headless match instead of a naive p1 strategy or the object-swap hack above. This is what `mobile/balance-sim.js` now does.
- **Playwright testing notes for this project, confirmed 2026-07-24:** (1) use `page.clock.runFor()`, not `fastForward()`, when a test needs to simulate elapsed time against a `setInterval`-based app timer (e.g. the phase countdown) — `fastForward` only fires a repeating interval once regardless of how much virtual time is passed, `runFor` fires every due callback. (2) Chromium is a valid substitute for *screenshotting* the politician-select carousel and end-of-game overlay (it hits the same ~980px no-viewport-tag fallback as Safari, so the visual result matches) — the existing webkit-only rule below still applies to anything touching the shared gameplay chrome. **Correction, same day, later in the session: the claim that these overlays were "authored directly at real mobile scale" was wrong — see `CLAUDE.md`'s 2.5x-scale convention bullet; that mismatch is exactly what caused the text-too-small bug this correction is attached to.** (3) If a WebKit `page.screenshot()` hangs on "waiting for fonts to load," don't just raise the timeout — that has happened in this environment even when the page itself (`document.fonts.status`, console, DOM) is already fully loaded and error-free; verify page state directly first, then fall back to Chromium per (2) if applicable. (4) Any Playwright DOM measurement on this app (`getBoundingClientRect()`, `clientHeight`, `window.innerHeight`) is reported in that same inflated ~980px canvas coordinate space, not real device px, even when you pass an explicit `viewport` matching a real device — multiply by `(real device CSS width / 980)` before comparing against a real device's available height/width budget. (5) Jawaharlal Nehru's special-power description (266 chars, `data/politicians-data.json`) is by far the longest of all 20 politicians' (next-longest is 129) — use his card (carousel index 9) as the standing worst-case layout check for any future ballot-card sizing change; if his card fits without scrolling, the rest reliably do too. (6) `object-fit:cover` on a full-bleed background image can crop noticeably tighter on a real phone than a Playwright screenshot predicts, even using the `devices['iPhone 14']` preset — confirmed 2026-07-25 building the welcome screen's poster background, where a real device sliced a face at the screen edge that had full margin in every emulated screenshot. Don't trust an emulated screenshot alone to judge crop tightness on a `cover` image where cropping the edges would cut off meaningful content (faces, text) — verify on a real device, or default to `object-fit:contain` instead. (7) **Headless Chromium does not replicate real mobile Safari's automatic ~0.4x zoom-out on this app** (see `CLAUDE.md`'s 2.5x-scale-convention bullet) — a Playwright screenshot taken at a real device viewport (e.g. 390×844) renders this app's UI looking severely oversized and overlapping, which is a testing artifact, not a real bug. Confirmed 2026-07-27: screenshotting `mobile/index.html`-family files at `viewport:{width:980, height:~2130}` (the app's own authored canvas size) instead produces layouts that visually match real-device screenshots. Use this ~980px-wide viewport, plus `getBoundingClientRect()` measurements against it, for any future headless self-check of this app's layout before showing changes to the user — this caught several real layout bugs (a flex `order` mistake, oversized floating panels) before they reached the user during the 2026-07-27 mobile HUD redesign session. (8) **That `~2130px` canvas height only matches real Safari with the address bar/toolbar hidden (home-screen-installed, or scrolled to hide) — a normal Safari tab (chrome visible) gives ~664px of real usable CSS height per item (4)'s device-height note, not 844px, which scales to only ~1669px in this app's ~980px-wide canvas.** Confirmed 2026-07-27: a "does this overflow" self-check of `index-redesign-a.html`'s politician-select cards at 980×2130 showed 0px overflow for all 21 politicians, but the user still reported Modi/Gandhi/Nehru's cards visibly cut off on their phone — re-testing at 980×1669 reproduced 187-327px of real overflow on exactly those cards. Any future "does this fit / does it ever need to scroll" self-check of a full-screen overlay must test at **both** heights (~2130 generous, ~1669 real-tab worst case) — the smaller one is what an ordinary (non-installed) Safari tab actually gives, and testing only at ~2130 can hide real, user-visible overflow bugs.
